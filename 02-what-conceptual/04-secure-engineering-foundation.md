STATE Engineering treats secure engineering as a foundational property of controlled change.

Security is not introduced as a separate lifecycle after functional implementation.

## Core rule

> **Security-relevant engineering principles are intrinsic to the STATE method and are applied throughout specification, transition design, realization, verification, modification and acceptance.**

## Methodological scope

The universal STATE core incorporates generally applicable and domain-neutral secure software and systems engineering principles and patterns.

The core is concerned with engineering method, including principles such as:

- clear abstractions and boundaries;
- modularity and reduced unnecessary complexity;
- least authority and least privilege;
- minimized unnecessary sharing and coupling;
- explicit mediation and permission;
- structured trust;
- secure defaults;
- secure evolvability;
- secure failure and recovery;
- accountability and traceability;
- repeatable and documented procedures;
- sufficient evidence;
- secure modification;
- minimization;
- verification appropriate to the claim.

## Outside the universal core

Domain-specific legal, regulatory, jurisdictional, mission, organizational or sector obligations are not universal STATE requirements merely because they may affect a particular implementation.

Such obligations can be introduced through Tailoring when a concrete use context requires them.

## Source discipline

STATE develops its own coherent normative principles rather than structuring the method as a compliance crosswalk.

Directly relevant secure software and systems engineering sources may inform the rationale for STATE requirements. The methodological source register records those sources and the engineering concepts absorbed from them.

The resulting STATE requirement remains a STATE requirement: the external source informs its engineering basis but does not become the structure of the method.
